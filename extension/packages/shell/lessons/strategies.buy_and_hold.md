`buy-and-hold` spends the cash on its first day, in equal parts, on every stock it can buy.
After that it never sells: new cash and the dividends you receive buy more of the same stocks.
It has no settings.

It is the yardstick. A strategy that trades more has to do better than this to be worth its
extra costs and its extra decisions, and often does not.
